To the Editor of *The Jewish Chronicle*

Sir,—The bare bones of a lecture (as of a chicken) are poor food even for controversialists, and I apologise for the summary character of my supplement to your report. It is, of course, unpleasant to realise that Achad Ha'am's premises should not have supported his own practice; but there is nothing fantastic or even unusual in that.

The most admirable of men have often said things which, when followed out in their full consequences, lead to results which they would have abhorred. Would Mazzini, with whom Achad Ha'am may perhaps not unjustly be compared, have approved of Mussolini? I yield not even to Sir Leon Simon (who wrote in your issue of last week) in my admiration for Achad Ha'am's personality and sentiments, and I certainly did not "attack" Achad Ha'am. I simply pointed out the logical consequences of his doctrine, which in other thinkers of the same trend, are commonly (and without acrimony) recognised.

The development of my own views on the subject is not of great importance; but as attention has been drawn to it I may observe that they were expressed fully in an address, afterwards published in the review "Gilyonoth," delivered on the 20th anniversary of Achad Ha'am's death in 1947.

Leon Roth.

3 Merton Street,  
Newnham, Cambridge.
