## His Doctrine Challenged

To the Editor of *The Jewish Chronicle*

Sir,—May I complete your report of my lecture on Achad Ha'am, which appeared in your issue of last week? I explained in some detail that (1) however much we may admire Achad Ha'am's moral outlook, we must realise that the theoretical foundation of his doctrine is both false and dangerous.

(2) This foundation is the conventional sociological one, and it cannot yield the absolutes (*e.g.*, absolute justice, or the absolute commitment of Jewry to the moral law) assumed by Achad Ha'am. Whence the dilemma: we can accept his general theory, in which case we have to let go his moral outlook; or accept his moral outlook, in which case his general theory must be dismissed.

(3) The generation brought up on Achad Ha'am accepted his general theory.

(4) Achad Ha'am himself stuck to his moral outlook, and on the occasion of one of the early instances of so-called retaliation published a letter to the press (which I read in my lecture) deploring and repudiating the new spirit, and ending with the Talmudic sentence: "If this is the Messiah, let him come and may I not see him."

Yet (5) Achad Ha'am added fuel to the moral treason of the Jewish intelligentsia by giving an account of Judaism as resting on a justice severed from love; and

(6) Achad Ha'am effectively led to the identification of the God of Judaism with the "national creative power" of Jewry or even the "national will to survive."

(7) Thus, ironically enough, Achad Ha'am's theories promoted the subordination of religion and morals to politics against which the Hebrew Bible is one continuous protest, and which has been made, alas, by so many even of our religious leaders the outstanding mark of the Judaism of our time.

These, I think, were my principal points in addition to those you mentioned. I may now add what I did not know last week, that all that I had to say on the subject is confirmed by an article of the well-known young Hebrew writer and novelist, Gideon Katznelson, published in the supplement of the Hebrew paper, *Haaretz*, of April 2. I direct to this article the careful attention of anyone who is still under the impression that there is in the mind of the young Israeli brought up on Achad Ha'am's principles any inherent connection between the State of Israel and what is commonly understood by the word Judaism. It may be that neither of them is any the worse for the dissociation; but the fact should be recognised and its consequences drawn.

Leon Roth.
